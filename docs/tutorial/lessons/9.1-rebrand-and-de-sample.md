# Lesson 9.1 — Rebrand & de-sample

> **Part 9 · Make it yours.** You've rebuilt an entire multi-tenant SaaS platform from an empty
> solution — every line understood, every decision examined. But it's still *Perezosoft*, and it
> still has a `Notes` sample slice sitting in it. This final lesson turns it into **your product** —
> and the first thing to learn is that you don't do that by editing the platform. The platform ships
> as packages; your product is a new app the template writes, which you then brand (there are more
> touchpoints than you'd guess, and the most-missed one is the transactional email), strip of the demo
> slice, and teach your domain's word for a tenant. A rebrand that *looks* done in the UI but ships
> emails with someone else's logo is a rebrand that isn't done, and the way you make "done"
> *verifiable* is the real lesson.

**Goal:** a new app from `dotnet new perezosoft-app`, with every brand touchpoint yours (name,
tagline, logo, colours, app id) — including the transactional emails and every localization file;
the `Notes` sample removed with an honest migration; the tenant term renamed in the app's pages and
strings; and mechanical checks that prove it.

**Concepts & principles:** product vs platform (ADR-034); what a template rename can and cannot do;
brand touchpoints as a checklist (not a vibe); why inline email is the most-missed spot; brand as
*configuration* of a package rather than edits to it; a completeness backstop (`git grep`); the
sample slice as scaffolding to delete; domain naming (the tenant term); scope discipline (the OUT
list); making "done" verifiable.

**Maps to:** `docs/NEW_APP_GUIDE.md` (Phases 2–3, "Removing the Notes sample") · `docs/REBRANDING.md`
· repo: `starter/` (what the template writes), `src/Core/PlatformBrand.cs` (`PlatformOptions.Brand`),
`src/Infrastructure/Email/BrandedEmail.cs`, `src/Shared.Ui/wwwroot/brand/*`,
`docs/brand/build_assets.py` (regenerates every raster from the SVG sources),
`src/Maui/Platforms/Android/SystemBarColors.cs` (the system-bar colours, A.1 §5),
`src/Host/Features/Notes/*` (to delete), `docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md` (the OUT list).

**Prerequisites:** 2.3 (`IEmailSender` + the branded email), 3.2 (the Notes slice you're now
deleting), 2.8 (the tenant/`Household` concept you're renaming), 3.5 (localization / the `.resx`
files that also carry the brand).

---

## 1. Motivate — your product is not a copy of the platform

The obvious move is to rename the repo you just built: find-and-replace `Perezosoft`, swap the
logo, ship. The first two products built on this platform did exactly that — cloned it, renamed 545
files, and then carried every platform fix across by hand, one port per app per fix, for months.
The copies were the same code twice, and the cost grew with apps × fixes (ADR-034's numbers). So the
platform changed shape: it ships as the `Perezosoft.Platform.*` packages, and a product is a **new,
small repo** the template writes — its host, its slices, its seeded UI, the platform's suites
inherited. A fix to the platform reaches it as a version bump.

```sh
dotnet new install Perezosoft.Platform.Templates::<version>
dotnet new perezosoft-app -n YourProduct -o yourproduct
```

The template renames its placeholder (`AppBrand`, `appbrand`, `APPBRAND`) to your name in every
identifier — projects, namespaces, the native app id, the OAuth scheme, the email brand's name, the
sender name. What it *can't* do is the rest of this lesson.

Then a user signs up, gets an OTP email — and it arrives with *Perezosoft's* logo, green palette and
tagline in the footer. The transactional emails are a **completely separate rendering path** from
the app: email HTML can't load your app's CSS or static assets (mail clients strip `<link>`, block
data-URIs, and ignore web fonts), so the branded email carries its *own* copy of the colours, logo
and tagline. It's the single most-forgotten surface in any rebrand, and it's the one your customers
see in their inbox. The lesson's real subject is therefore not "how to change a colour" but **how to
know you're actually done** across a surface area larger than it looks.

## 2. The checklist — every touchpoint, grouped

`docs/REBRANDING.md` enumerates what the template left you. It clusters into four groups:

1. **Brand assets** — the RCL brand assets (SVG source + the PNG lockups the UI actually references —
   see §3, and the lockups carry the **tagline**), the web favicons/OG image/PWA icons, the MAUI
   launcher icon + splash, and the **colour palette**: the semantic `:root` tokens in `app.css`
   (single-sourced for web *and* native) and the Android system bars (`colors.xml`,
   `SystemBarColors`), which must agree with it.
2. **The brand options** — one block in `AppComposition.ConfigurePlatform`: `options.Brand.Tagline`,
   `Colors`, `Logo` (a PNG) and `Strings` (your resx for the few email keys that name the platform).
   The template already set `options.Brand.Name`, and the name flows from there into the email header
   and footer, the **MFA issuer** (what authenticator apps display — users only ever see it inside
   Google Authenticator & co.), the OpenAPI title and the telemetry service name. Plus the product
   copy in both `AppStrings` `.resx` files.
3. **Your tenant noun** — §6.
4. **The native app's identity** — the `ApplicationId` the template wrote (`com.yourproduct.app`) is
   the store identity: settle it before the first release. Change it and two more places move with
   it in the same commit: the **`tests/native-smoke-android/smoke.js` `PKG` fallback** and the
   **MacCatalyst `Entitlements.plist` keychain-access group** (miss the second and SecureStorage
   silently fails on Catalyst under real signing). The OAuth callback scheme already agrees across
   the five files that name it; register `{scheme}://auth` with the provider consoles.

Notice group 2. In the clone era the email's palette, logo and tagline were constants at the top of
`BrandedEmail.cs`, and the checklist had to nudge you there four times. Now the mails belong to the
Infrastructure package, and an app never edits a package: it *configures* it (ADR-034 point 4 —
options first). The brand became data (`src/Core/PlatformBrand.cs`), validated at startup — a colour that
isn't `#rrggbb` or a logo that isn't a PNG stops the host before a single mail goes out.

## 3. Three gotchas the checklist encodes

Beyond the enumeration, three non-obvious traps are worth internalizing because they *look* fine and
silently aren't:

- **The UI references the PNG lockups, not the SVGs.** A wordmark SVG embedded via `<img>` won't
  load your webfont, so its text silently falls back to Helvetica/Arial — your logo, wrong typeface,
  and no error. So the SVGs are the *editable source*, the PNGs are *rendered from them*, and
  `Login.razor`/`Home.razor` point at the **PNGs**. Replace the SVG, re-render the PNG, or the logo
  looks subtly broken.
- **The email logo must be a PNG, CID-embedded.** `BrandedEmail` embeds the logo as a
  multipart/related part — the one approach Gmail *and* Outlook render reliably; they block
  data-URIs and strip SVG. Ship a ~128px square PNG as an embedded resource of your host and hand
  its bytes to `options.Brand.Logo`.
- **Rasters are generated, never hand-exported.** Both traps above resolve into one command:
  `python docs/brand/build_assets.py` renders every PNG, the `.ico`, the PWA/OG/Apple-touch set,
  the store icons *and* the email logo from the SVG sources in `src/Shared.Ui/wwwroot/brand/` — with
  a headless Chromium, so lockup wordmarks carry the real webfont (REBRANDING §1). The platform's own
  2026-09 face swap (#7) is the worked example: six SVG edits, one run, 22 files changed, 16 of them
  rasters nobody opened in an editor — including the email logo that a hand rebrand forgets. The
  template doesn't ship the script: copy it into your app's `docs/brand/`.

These are the kind of details that turn a "quick rebrand" into an afternoon of confusion — which is
exactly why they live in a written checklist, not in someone's memory.

## 4. The completeness backstop — make "done" a command

Here's the lesson's sharpest idea. After working the checklist, you don't *hope* it's complete —
you **prove** it:

```sh
git grep -i appbrand                                    # the template's placeholder → nothing
git grep -i "Lazy reputation. Efficient engineering."   # the platform's tagline → nothing
git grep -i -e "#465d4d" -e "#6b8a72"                   # the platform's palette → nothing
```

If any returns a hit, you missed a spot, and it tells you exactly where. This turns "is the
rebrand done?" from a subjective judgment into a **mechanical, repeatable check** — the same instinct
as the coverage gate, the arch tests, and the RLS parity gate throughout this course: *don't rely on
diligence when a command can verify.*

> **One expectation to set: `git grep -i perezosoft` is not part of the check.** Run it in your new
> app and it returns hits — `using Perezosoft.Infrastructure…`, `Perezosoft.Platform.Api` in the
> project files, the `perezosoft` feed in `nuget.config`. Those are the **packages' names**, not
> brand copy a user ever sees, and they stay in every app by design: an app that renamed them would
> reference packages that don't exist. The template knows the difference (it renames only its
> placeholder), and so does the ownership gate: the map's `keep` list names the tokens no brand map
> may rewrite.

Pair it with a functional check the grep can't do — trigger a
real OTP or invite email and confirm it arrives with your logo, colours, name, and tagline — and
"done" is now concrete, falsifiable steps instead of a vibe. A rebrand you can *verify* is a
rebrand you can *trust*.

## 5. De-sample — delete the `Notes` slice cleanly

The `Notes` feature (3.2) was scaffolding — the reference vertical slice that taught the pattern.
Your product replaces it with real features, so it goes when your first slice lands. Deleting it
cleanly means removing the whole slice *and* its wiring, in one commit — and `git grep DELETE-ME`
finds every line, because the sample was built to be removed:

- the `src/Host/Features/Notes/` directory (handler, endpoints, models, `NotesDataContributor`);
- its wiring in `AppComposition.cs` (the `AddScoped` lines in `AddAppServices`, `app.MapNotes()` in
  `MapAppEndpoints`), its `DbSet<Note>` in `AppDbContext`, its `NoteConfiguration.cs`, its entries in
  `tests/Api.Tests/App/AppAllowlists.cs` and `AppTestComposition.cs`;
- the `NotesSliceTests` and the Postman folder.

The `Note` *entity* itself stays where it is — it ships in the Core package, so the sample builds in
every app; once nothing maps it, it's just an unused type. What must not be left behind is the
**schema**: with the `DbSet` and configuration gone, the EF model no longer has a `Notes` table but
the last migration's snapshot does, and `dotnet ef migrations has-pending-model-changes` reports the
drift. The honest fix is a **`RemoveNotesSample` migration** — `dotnet ef migrations add
RemoveNotesSample --project src/Host` scaffolds the `DropTable` and re-syncs the snapshot. Don't
squash or delete the migrations that created it: a migration that already ran anywhere is history,
and your app owns its history from its first migration on (ADR-034 point 5). After deleting and
migrating, the build + arch tests + the schema gate all still pass — which is the proof the removal
was clean (no dangling reference, no orphaned registration, no model drift). Then you build your
first *real* slice by copying the pattern Notes taught.

## 6. Rename the tenant term — speak your domain

The platform's tenant concept ships with the app-facing label **Household** — but your domain has
its own word: *Workspace*, *Team*, *Organization*, *Practice*, *Band*. Renaming it in your pages and
strings makes the product speak your users' language. Crucially, two things stay:

- The *code* term stays **tenant** (the internal abstraction is domain-agnostic on purpose).
  `ITenantScoped`, the query filter, and RLS never mention "Household," so they never need to know you
  now call it a "Workspace." The abstraction earning its keep one final time.
- The **API** stays `api/household` too (ADR-034 point 6). The controllers are in the package, and
  the package is every app's: renaming its routes would mean a fork, which is the thing the packages
  exist to prevent. Your app renames its **seed** — the `Household_*` strings in both languages, the
  page and its route, the header link, the `nav-household` test id and the journeys that use it —
  and nothing your users never see.

One subtlety you may hit, and it's instructive: occasionally the *label* leaked into an internal
identifier that *should* have used the abstraction — e.g. an enum member named `NoHousehold` deep in
a service. When your rename touches something that isn't user-facing copy, that's a signal: it was
miscategorized — an internal name wearing the app-facing word — and the fix belongs upstream, in the
platform, renamed to the **code term** (`NoTenant`). The clean spots rename effortlessly (they were
properly abstracted); the one that resists is telling you where an earlier shortcut blurred the
label/abstraction line.

## 7. Scope discipline — the OUT list

One more habit for making it yours: before building a feature, check the **"OUT" list in
`docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`**. Every project accumulates "wouldn't it be nice" ideas; a written
*out-of-scope* list is how you keep the build focused and how you make deferral a *decision* rather
than a drift. Adding something the brief explicitly deferred should require an explicit change to the
brief — the same "don't silently reverse a settled decision" discipline as the ADR log (0.1). The
platform models this: features like per-record ACLs (6.1) and the non-web framework commitment are
*named as deferred*, not forgotten. Making it yours means keeping that discipline as you extend.

## 8. Red — the rebrand is verifiable

```gherkin
Scenario: no placeholder and no platform brand survives
  When I run the placeholder, tagline and palette searches
  Then all three return nothing

Scenario: a real email carries the new brand
  When I trigger an OTP or invite email
  Then it arrives with the new logo (CID PNG), palette, name, and tagline

Scenario: the sample is gone and the build is still clean
  When Features/Notes and its wiring are deleted and RemoveNotesSample is added
  Then build + arch tests + the schema gate all pass (no dangling reference, no model drift)
```

There's no new xUnit here — the "tests" are a grep, an inbox, and a green build. That's fitting: the
lesson is about *verifiable completeness* of a cross-cutting change, and the tools that verify it are
the mechanical checks, not assertions in a test project.

## 9. Run it

```sh
dotnet new perezosoft-app -n YourProduct -o yourproduct && cd yourproduct
# work docs/REBRANDING.md (in the platform) top to bottom, then:
git grep -i appbrand                   # must be empty
dotnet build && dotnet test tests/Api.Tests   # green after deleting Features/Notes + RemoveNotesSample
# trigger an OTP email → confirm your logo/colours/name/tagline in a real inbox
```

## 10. Architecture Decision

> **The fork:** how is the platform turned into a distinct product, completely and verifiably?
> (a) Clone the platform and change what's visible in the running app; (b) clone it and
> find-and-replace the brand everywhere; (c) a new app from the template, which renames its own
> placeholder, plus a written, grouped checklist of what no rename reaches (the brand assets, the
> email brand as options, the tenant noun, the app identity), backed by `git grep` completeness
> checks + a real-email functional check, plus a signposted sample-slice deletion.
>
> **Chosen:** (c) (`docs/NEW_APP_GUIDE.md`, `docs/REBRANDING.md`, ADR-034). The template draws the
> line between your identifiers and the packages' names exactly once, so nothing has to; branding
> spans surfaces that don't share code (app CSS vs inline email vs native manifests vs `.resx` copy
> vs OAuth registrations), so only an enumerated checklist covers them; the greps make completeness
> *mechanical*; the real-email check catches the most-missed surface functionally; the
> `🗑️ DELETE-ME` markers make de-sampling a clean, verifiable removal.
>
> **Rejected:** (a) — "looks rebranded" ships someone else's logo in every transactional email, the
> exact §1 failure, and a clone carries the platform's code twice. (b) — what the first two products
> did: 545 files rewritten, the package names renamed along with the brand, and every platform fix a
> port per app from then on; a blind replace also misses the logo binaries and would rewrite the
> *code* term `tenant` along with the label.
>
> **The trade:** the app can't change a package's code — a fix it needs goes upstream as a seam
> (ADR-034 point 4), and a release reaches it only when it bumps. Accepted: that constraint is
> precisely what makes a platform fix one release instead of a port per app.

## 11. Checkpoint

```sh
git add -A && git commit -m "chore: brand YourProduct, delete the Notes sample, rename the tenant label"
git tag lesson-9.1
```

rebuilt line by line, decision by decision, and a product of your own standing on it.

You should now be able to: explain why a product is a new app on the packages rather than a copy of

via the OUT list.
